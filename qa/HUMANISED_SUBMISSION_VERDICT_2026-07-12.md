# Humanised Submission Verdict
Date: 2026-07-12

Valentine, this is not a bad paper. It is a dangerous paper if submitted under the wrong name.

The science is strongest when it is honest about what the data can and cannot do. You have a corrected, reproducible Ghana HIV spatial workflow. You have a real south-north regional signal. You have useful sensitivity work showing that some spatial structure survives when the model is restricted to genuinely district-level covariates. That is worth publishing.

But it is not a district-level HIV determinant paper. The outcome is regional DHS HIV prevalence copied down to districts. VCT uptake and wife-beating acceptance are also regional DHS estimates. That means the maps are district-boundary visualisations of regional patterns, not proof that one district differs from its neighbour. Reviewers will forgive that if you say it first. They will punish it if they feel they discovered it.

My editorial call: do one more revision round. Then submit to PLOS Global Public Health first. Cambridge Epidemiology & Infection is possible, but it will need a sharper infectious-disease surveillance argument and a less ambitious district-level framing.

Best framing:
"A transparent ecological spatial mapping and sensitivity analysis of Ghana's regional DHS HIV estimates rendered across district boundaries."

Avoid:
"District-level determinants of HIV prevalence in Ghana."

Submission odds after revision:
- PLOS Global Public Health: about 45-60%.
- Epidemiology & Infection: about 25-35%.

The one thing to fix before submission: make the limitation title-level. Put it in the title/subtitle, first abstract sentence, dashboard caveat, poster background box, and cover letter. Then the paper reads as careful. Without that, it reads overclaimed.
