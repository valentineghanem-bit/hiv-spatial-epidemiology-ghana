## Cambridge Summary Draft
HIV prevalence in Ghana varies geographically, but public datasets rarely support direct district-level HIV inference. This ecological spatial study mapped 2014 Ghana Demographic and Health Survey (DHS) regional HIV biomarker estimates onto 261 census district records and 260 Ghana Statistical Service boundary polygons, alongside 2022 DHS behavioural indicators and 2021 Census district socioeconomic covariates. We estimated Global Moran's I, Local Indicators of Spatial Association, Getis-Ord Gi*, spatial lag regression, LASSO, and Random Forest/SHAP models, with sensitivity analyses restricted to genuinely district-varying predictors. HIV prevalence showed strong positive spatial autocorrelation (Moran's I = 0.920; permutation p = 0.001), with 59 High-High and 51 Low-Low local clusters. Random Forest spatial leave-one-region-out cross-validation achieved R2 = 0.611; full-model SHAP values ranked voluntary counselling and testing uptake and wife-beating acceptance highest, but both are DHS regional pass-through variables. District-only sensitivity models retained predictive signal but shifted importance to latitude and Census-derived socioeconomic indicators. Findings support region-level HIV testing and surveillance prioritisation in Ghana's southern belt, not named-district targeting, until district-resolved surveillance or small-area-estimation evidence is available.
