Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider our Original Paper, `Regional HIV Spatial Patterns Mapped Across Ghanaian District Boundaries`, for publication in Epidemiology & Infection.

This ecological spatial study examines Ghana's regional DHS HIV biomarker estimates mapped across national district boundaries, linked with DHS behavioural indicators and district-varying Census socioeconomic covariates. Its contribution is not a claim of independent district HIV surveillance. Instead, the paper provides a transparent national spatial atlas and modelling audit showing where public aggregate data support region-level HIV programme prioritisation, and where district-specific inference would require small-area estimation or district-resolved surveillance.

The paper should interest the journal's readers because it addresses infectious-disease spatial epidemiology in a lower-middle-income setting, combines classical spatial statistics with interpretable machine learning, and is deliberately explicit about the modifiable areal unit problem and regional granularity of DHS-derived HIV indicators. We believe this transparency strengthens the paper and makes it useful for public-health decision makers who often need to interpret subnational maps built from mixed-resolution data.

All analyses use publicly available, aggregate, de-identified secondary data. No individual-level data were accessed. The manuscript follows STROBE guidance for cross-sectional/ecological observational studies. The author declares no competing interests and no specific funding for this research.
